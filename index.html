Brainwave Entrainment

Two tones. One perceived beat.
Your brain follows.

When slightly different frequencies reach each ear, say 400 Hz left, 407 Hz right, your brain synthesizes a 7 Hz "beat" in the superior olivary complex. Evidence suggests this may nudge neural oscillations toward theta waves linked to relaxation, or gamma waves associated with focus and cognition.

Ingendoh et al. (2023). Binaural Beats to Entrain the Brain? A Systematic Review. PLOS ONE.
The Awe Response

Awe quiets stress.
Down to the cellular level.

Awe activates the vagus nerve, reduces sympathetic arousal, and lowers inflammatory cytokines including IL-6. Music is among the most reliable awe elicitors, and research identifies five distinct pathways through which awe produces measurable improvements in both mental and physical health.

Monroy & Keltner (2023). Awe as a Pathway to Mental and Physical Health. Perspectives on Psychological Science.
Music-Evoked Chills

Those chills you feel?
That's dopamine.

Roughly 90% of people experience frisson, the involuntary shiver music can produce at moments of peak beauty. It activates the nucleus accumbens, the brain's reward center, releasing dopamine in the same cascade linked to pleasure, transcendence, and awe. Music is one of its most reliable triggers.

de Fleurian & Pearce (2021). Chills in Music: A Systematic Review. Psychological Bulletin.
The Small Self

Feeling small, in the best way, is good for you.

Awe produces a quieting of the ego researchers call "self-diminishment," a more accurate self-view that generates genuine humility. Awe-prone people are rated as more humble by the people who know them best, and this humility predicts better relationships, greater prosociality, and lasting improvements in well-being.

Stellar et al. (2018). Awe and Humility. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.
